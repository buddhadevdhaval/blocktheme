Understanding Results

What your result actually means

Results from hereditary cancer genetic testing fall into one of three categories. Here's what each one means in plain language.

Negative

A negative result is reassuring, but since risk is also based on personal and family history, discuss next steps with your provider.

Depending on their clinical history, genetic testing may still be advisable for family members.

Positive

A positive result confirms an increased cancer risk and may be the basis for specific medical management recommendations tied to that gene.

There may be up to a 50/50 chance that your close family members carry the same mutation. Testing for them may be recommended.

You choose whether to receive secondary findings. Before testing, you can decide in advance whether you want to be informed of secondary findings if they are identified. There is no obligation to receive this information, and your primary testing results are not affected by your choice. Discuss the options with your healthcare provider or genetic counselor.
